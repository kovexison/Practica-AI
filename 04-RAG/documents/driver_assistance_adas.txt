TITLE: Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS)
CATEGORY: Electronics and Safety
AUDIENCE: Sales and support staff
NOTE: Simplified training material. Figures are illustrative and not official specifications.

WHAT ADAS MEANS

Advanced Driver Assistance Systems support the driver. They do not replace the driver. Every system
described here requires the driver to remain attentive and legally responsible at all times.

SENSORS

Three sensor types work together. Cameras read lane markings, traffic signs, pedestrians and vehicle
shapes; they are rich in detail but degrade in fog, heavy rain and direct low sun. Radar measures
distance and closing speed accurately and is largely unaffected by weather, but is poor at identifying
what an object actually is. Ultrasonic sensors cover very short distances and are used for parking.

Combining them is called sensor fusion. Fusion matters because each sensor fails differently. Radar may
detect a stationary metal object that a camera cannot classify, while a camera can read a speed limit
sign that radar cannot perceive at all.

ADAPTIVE CRUISE CONTROL

Adaptive cruise control maintains a set speed and a selected following distance. When the vehicle ahead
slows, the system decelerates. When the road clears, it returns to the set speed. Stop and go capability
extends this to full stops in traffic; if the stop lasts under 30 seconds the vehicle resumes
automatically, and beyond that the driver must confirm.

LANE KEEPING AND STEERING ASSIST

Lane departure warning alerts the driver when the vehicle drifts across a marking without indicating.
Lane keeping assistant applies gentle steering correction to guide the vehicle back. Steering and lane
control assistant goes further and continuously centres the vehicle within its lane.

These systems depend on clear lane markings. On worn markings, in roadworks with conflicting lines, or
in snow, availability drops and the system will hand control back. Hands-off detection ensures the
driver stays engaged; ignoring escalating warnings causes the system to slow the vehicle and stop safely.

COLLISION WARNING AND AUTOMATIC BRAKING

Forward collision warning monitors closing speed to the vehicle ahead and warns visually and audibly.
If the driver does not react, automatic emergency braking intervenes. The system is designed to reduce
severity, and it cannot prevent every collision. Effectiveness falls as speed rises, because the
distance needed to shed energy grows with the square of speed.

BLIND SPOT AND CROSS TRAFFIC

Blind spot detection uses rear-facing radar to monitor the adjacent lanes and illuminates a warning in
the door mirror. If the indicator is active while a vehicle occupies that space, the warning escalates.
Rear cross traffic alert warns of vehicles approaching from the side while reversing out of a space,
which is valuable where parked vehicles block the driver's view.

PARKING ASSISTANCE

Parking assistant measures gaps while driving past and then controls steering, and in later versions
also acceleration and braking, to manoeuvre into the space. Reversing assistant records the last 50
metres travelled forward and can retrace that path in reverse, which is useful in narrow lanes.

LIMITATIONS THAT MUST BE COMMUNICATED

Sensors can be blinded by dirt, ice, snow or heavy spray, and the vehicle will report reduced
availability. Windscreen replacement requires camera recalibration; an uncalibrated camera may appear to
function while judging distances incorrectly. Aftermarket accessories mounted near sensors can obstruct
them. Systems are validated against typical traffic scenarios and may not respond correctly to unusual
situations such as an overturned vehicle or debris in the roadway.
