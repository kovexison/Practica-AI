HOW THE RANGE IS ORGANISED

The training fleet is organised into three families. Compact models are front or all wheel drive and
prioritise interior space in a small footprint. Sedan and touring models are rear biased and prioritise
driving balance. Sports activity vehicles are raised, mostly all wheel drive, and prioritise versatility.

Even numbers denote coupe and convertible derivatives of the odd numbered sedan they are based on. A
higher leading number means a larger vehicle, not necessarily a faster one.

REPRESENTATIVE MODELS

The C1 compact hatchback is the entry point, with a 1.5 litre three cylinder turbo petrol engine
producing 115 kW, front wheel drive and a kerb weight of 1,420 kilograms.

The S3 sedan is the volume model, offered with a 2.0 litre four cylinder turbo at 135 kW or a 3.0 litre
inline six turbo at 210 kW, rear wheel drive as standard and all wheel drive optional.

The S5 touring adds an extended luggage compartment of 570 litres, rising to 1,700 litres with the rear
seats folded, and is offered as a plug-in hybrid with 65 kilometres of electric range.

The X3 sports activity vehicle uses the same 2.0 litre four cylinder as the S3 with all wheel drive as
standard and offers a towing capacity of 2,000 kilograms braked.

The E4 is fully electric, with an 81.2 kWh usable battery, dual motors producing a combined 290 kW, all
wheel drive and a quoted range of 480 kilometres on the standard test cycle.

The M6 is the performance flagship, with a 4.4 litre V8 twin turbo producing 390 kW, all wheel drive
with a rear biased default split, and adaptive dampers as standard.

CHOOSING BETWEEN THEM

Buyers covering high annual mileage on motorways still benefit from diesel or from the inline six
petrol. Buyers driving predominantly in town with home charging available are better served by the E4.
Buyers with a mixed pattern and no home charging should avoid the plug-in hybrid, since its advantage
depends entirely on being charged regularly.

Towing requirements point towards the X3, and load volume towards the S5 touring. The M6 is bought for
performance rather than for practicality, and its running costs reflect that.
