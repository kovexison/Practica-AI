TITLE: Vehicle Safety Systems
CATEGORY: Safety
AUDIENCE: General staff
NOTE: Simplified training material.

PASSIVE AND ACTIVE SAFETY

Passive safety reduces harm once a collision is unavoidable. Active safety works to prevent the
collision happening at all. A modern vehicle depends on both, and the two are increasingly linked:
sensors that detect an imminent impact can tighten seat belts and close windows before it occurs.

BODY STRUCTURE

The passenger compartment is a rigid safety cell built from high strength and hot formed steels. Around
it sit deliberately softer crumple zones that deform in a controlled way, extending the duration of the
impact and lowering the peak force reaching occupants.

Electric vehicles place the battery in a reinforced enclosure within the floor. This lowers the centre of
gravity, which substantially reduces rollover risk, and the enclosure itself contributes to side impact
stiffness.

RESTRAINTS

Seat belts remain the single most effective safety device in any vehicle. Pretensioners remove slack at
the start of an impact and load limiters then allow controlled belt payout to avoid chest injury.

Airbags supplement belts and are not a substitute for them. A front airbag deploys in milliseconds and
is designed for an occupant already restrained by a belt. Side and curtain airbags protect the torso and
head in side impacts and during rollover.

Rear facing child seats must never be fitted to a seat with an active front airbag. The passenger airbag
must be deactivated first.

ELECTRONIC STABILITY CONTROL

Stability control compares the driver's steering input with the vehicle's actual rotation. When the
vehicle begins to deviate, the system brakes individual wheels and may reduce engine torque to bring the
path back under control. It cannot overcome physics; it works within the grip the tyres can provide.

Anti-lock braking prevents wheel lock so that steering remains possible during emergency braking.
Traction control limits wheel spin under acceleration.

HIGH VOLTAGE SAFETY

High voltage systems operate at several hundred volts and are lethal if handled incorrectly. Orange
cabling identifies high voltage circuits and must never be cut, pierced or disconnected by untrained
personnel.

The system continuously monitors insulation resistance and isolates the battery automatically if a fault
or a crash is detected. Only technicians with high voltage qualification may work on these systems, and
only after formal de-energisation and verification.

Damaged batteries carry a risk of delayed thermal runaway, which can begin hours after an incident.
Affected vehicles are quarantined outdoors at a defined distance from buildings and other vehicles.

EMERGENCY CALL

The vehicle can place an emergency call automatically when a severe impact is detected, transmitting
location and basic vehicle data. It can also be triggered manually. The system requires mobile network
coverage to function.
