TITLE: Service and Maintenance Schedule
CATEGORY: Aftersales
AUDIENCE: Service advisors
NOTE: Simplified training material. Intervals are illustrative and not official specifications.

CONDITION BASED SERVICE

Maintenance is not driven by a fixed calendar alone. Sensors monitor operating conditions and the
vehicle calculates remaining service life for each item independently. A vehicle used for short cold
journeys will need attention sooner than one used for long steady journeys over the same distance.

The driver sees a due date per item in the vehicle menu. Advisors should read these values rather than
assuming a fixed interval.

TYPICAL INTERVALS

Engine oil and filter are replaced at up to 30,000 kilometres or every 24 months, whichever comes first,
and often sooner under demanding use. Brake fluid is replaced every 24 months regardless of distance,
because it absorbs moisture from the air, which lowers its boiling point and risks vapour lock under
heavy braking. Cabin microfilter is replaced every 30,000 kilometres or 24 months. Spark plugs are
replaced at approximately 60,000 kilometres for turbocharged petrol engines.

Brake pads and discs are wear items with no fixed interval. Sensors report remaining pad life based on
measured wear. Front pads typically last between 25,000 and 60,000 kilometres depending on driving style
and terrain.

ELECTRIC VEHICLE DIFFERENCES

Electric vehicles have no engine oil, no spark plugs, no exhaust system and no gearbox oil service on a
single speed reduction gear. Maintenance cost is correspondingly lower.

Brake wear is markedly lower because regenerative braking performs most routine deceleration. This
creates its own problem: discs that are rarely used can corrode. Periodic firm braking, or the vehicle's
automatic brake cleaning function, keeps the surfaces clear.

Items that remain unchanged include tyres, cabin filter, brake fluid, suspension components and wiper
blades. Tyre wear can be higher than on a comparable combustion vehicle because of greater mass and
instant torque delivery.

The high-voltage battery coolant is checked according to the vehicle's own schedule and must never be
topped up with ordinary coolant, as the specification differs.

TYRES

Recommended pressures are on the door pillar label and vary by load and by tyre size. Pressure should be
checked cold, because driving raises temperature and therefore pressure. Under-inflation increases
rolling resistance, raises consumption, and causes shoulder wear and heat build-up.

Tread depth must meet the legal minimum, commonly 1.6 millimetres, but wet grip degrades well before
that point and replacement around 3 millimetres is advisable. Tyres age even when unused; replacement
after approximately six years is recommended irrespective of tread depth.

DIAGNOSTICS AND SOFTWARE

Fault memory is read during every service visit. Many faults are intermittent and stored without
illuminating a warning lamp, and reading the memory reveals developing problems early.

Software updates are delivered over the air or installed during a service visit. Updates address
infotainment behaviour, driver assistance calibration and charging performance. The vehicle must not be
driven while an update is installing and should have sufficient charge or fuel to complete it.

WARNING LAMP COLOURS

Red indicates a serious fault; stop safely as soon as it is possible to do so. Yellow or amber indicates
a fault that needs attention but permits careful continued driving. Green and blue are informational and
confirm that a system is active.
